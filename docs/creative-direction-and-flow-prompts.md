# AZURE — photography, product collection and scroll-motion brief

Status: production brief for the next design pass. These assets and interactions are not yet implemented in the published page. New AZURE concept designs were selected by the user. Product names below are creative working names, not a confirmed catalog.

## What is missing in the current page

- A photographic camera gallery. The existing mountains are CSS illustrations; changing their tint or scale does not demonstrate a real camera.
- Distinct product photography for every phone. One Pro uses one repeated render; One still uses a CSS phone. There is no third phone design.
- An accessories collection: charger, cable, charging stand, earbuds and cases.
- Product cards that expose images, color choice, compatibility, details and a clear action together.
- Scroll-controlled product motion. The current tilt and decorative illustrations do not provide a cinematic product walkthrough.
- A real design film. The current design-story button opens explanatory text.
- A visual rhythm beyond dark feature blocks: immersive dark product reveals, quieter graphite product shelves and full-width photographic moments.

## Proposed page sequence

1. Header: Phones / Camera / Design / Accessories / Compare. Keep mobile menu and bag accessible.
2. Hero: Beyond Limits. One Pro reveal, one primary Explore phones action and one secondary design-film action when a film exists.
3. Product design story: a short pinned scroll sequence, rotating the phone from rear to front. Text advances at three meaningful moments: finish, cameras, display.
4. Camera: actual photographic landscape, portrait, night and macro images. Each category gets its own image; no simulated zoom presented as different optical performance.
5. Performance and display: restrained still imagery and readable details. No invented processor benchmark, brightness or refresh-rate claims.
6. Charging: one short scroll-controlled charger/cable shot, followed by a still that remains useful without motion.
7. Phones: One Pro, One Air and One, all with distinct product images. Model, finish options, detail view and compare selection. Show prices only when supplied or explicitly marked illustrative.
8. Accessories: category filters All / Charging / Audio / Protection. Product cards with real image assets, names, color options and compatibility notes. Desktop grid; mobile swipeable shelves with visible next-card edges and previous/next buttons. Do not turn ordinary page scrolling into sideways scrolling.
9. Everyday photography: one full-width lifestyle image of the device in use, with minimal copy.
10. Comparison, product contents, FAQ and footer. Only describe availability, warranty and included accessories when confirmed.

## How to prepare references in Google Flow

Generate and select one still for each product before generating its video. Use the selected image again as the product reference in all related clips. Do not regenerate the product from scratch for every shot.

Flow supports image references and start/end frames. Available controls depend on the selected model and plan. Check the available duration, resolution and aspect ratio before generating. Sources:

- https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353334
- https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16352836

Production targets, not guaranteed settings: 8-second clips, 16:9 desktop framing, the highest useful available resolution. A separate 9:16 composition is preferable for narrow screens; do not assume a wide crop will work. Keep the original downloaded files. No need to extract frames yourself.

## Common direction for every motion prompt

Use the approved product reference image. Preserve its exact silhouette, lens count, ports, buttons, colors and material finishes throughout. One continuous take. Smooth slow movement, consistent exposure and focus, crisp frames suitable for scroll scrubbing. No cuts, transitions, rapid motion blur, morphing, duplicated parts, pulsing light, added typography, interface overlays, sound-dependent storytelling or new logos. Reserve clean space for website copy, which will be added separately. Avoid introducing details not visible in the supplied references.

### V01 — Hero rotation / essential

Use the approved AZURE One Pro reference as the exact product identity. Create an eight-second photorealistic luxury product shot on a near-black seamless background. Place one phone in the right half of a wide composition, leaving the left 40 percent clean. Start at a rear three-quarter angle with all three camera lenses visible. Hold briefly, then rotate the rigid phone slowly around its vertical axis toward the provided front-view reference. Reveal the metal side rail and end at a front three-quarter angle with a restrained static violet abstract wallpaper. Soft cool key light, delicate violet rim, stable camera and product scale. The phone remains entirely within frame. One continuous movement, no cuts, no changing lens count, no text, no flicker, minimal blur. Hold the final pose briefly.

Reference needs: approved rear view and a matching front view. Intended web behavior: pin the stage for a short scroll segment, map scroll progress to image-frame progress, and retain ordinary page navigation.

### V02 — Camera and material macro / secondary

Use the approved AZURE One Pro camera macro reference. Create an eight-second photorealistic macro product shot on near-black. Begin with the complete triple-camera island visible. Move the camera slowly sideways and slightly closer across the lens rims and satin rear surface. End with one large lens and portions of the two adjacent lenses still visible, maintaining the reference geometry. A broad soft reflection moves naturally over the glass as the view changes. Keep optical elements crisp and metal reflections controlled. Consistent focus without pulsing. No lens movement, extra components, magical effects, captions, cuts or heavy blur. Smooth continuous motion suitable for reversible scroll scrubbing.

Intended web behavior: a short close-up passage followed by the real-photo gallery. The gallery itself stays directly browseable and does not require scrolling through a film.

### V03 — Charger and cable / essential

Use the approved One Pro and braided cable references, with a separate charger reference for the background. Create an eight-second photorealistic charging demonstration. One phone rests on a dark studio surface at a shallow angle; its bottom USB-C port is clearly visible and stationary. The matching cable connector approaches slowly in a straight line aligned with the port, then seats gently and stops. The cable bends naturally with no stretching or intersections. Keep the charger still and softly visible in the background, matching its reference. A small restrained light on the phone indicates connection without showing percentages or charging-speed claims. Locked camera, cool softbox light, stable exposure. No cuts, sparks, extra ports, hands, labels or geometry changes. Hold the connected state at the end.

Reference needs: a clear phone bottom/port view as well as the cable. Reject results where the connector passes through the body or the port moves. If a stable insertion cannot be generated, use a slow camera move around an already connected still instead.

### V04 — Product assembly / optional concept sequence

Create an eight-second photorealistic conceptual assembly animation using approved AZURE One Pro front and rear references. Start with three clean exterior layers aligned on a single axis: front glass/display assembly, metal chassis and violet rear panel with its camera module attached. Keep the camera position fixed. The three rigid layers slowly travel straight toward each other, align precisely and settle into one complete phone. End at the approved rear three-quarter pose. Near-black studio, soft cool reflections and restrained violet edge light. Stable silhouettes, correct outer dimensions, no bending, no melting, no extra parts, no flying screws, no cuts, no text. Do not expose invented circuitry, battery construction or engineering labels. Hold the completed phone briefly.

This is a visual concept, not an accurate teardown. Use only if the generated parts remain coherent; otherwise replace it with the exterior rotation. It can replace V02, rather than adding another long pinned sequence.

## Scroll implementation contract after assets are supplied

- Downloaded clips will be inspected before integration; prompts do not guarantee geometric consistency or frame accuracy.
- Extract sequential frames from accepted clips and bind displayed frame to scroll progress. Scrolling back reverses the progression; stopping scrolling holds the frame.
- Prefer two primary sequences: hero and charging. Use camera or assembly only where it adds information.
- Begin with a sharp poster image; progressively load nearby frames. Do not preload every full-resolution clip at page load.
- Keep phone scale readable on mobile. Use a separate mobile composition or a confirmed safe crop.
- Reduced-motion mode shows the informative still and all accompanying text. All content remains reachable with keyboard navigation.
- Provide a visible way to continue past long product stories. Do not block scrolling or require motion to use product filters, comparison or the bag.
- For the optional design-film button, use actual playback controls and an explicitly opened film. Do not relabel the existing text dialog as a video.
